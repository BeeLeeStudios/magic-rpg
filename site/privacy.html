Dragons vs Math – Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 26, 2026

Dragons vs Math ("the app") is a math practice game for children ages 5–13, published by BeeLee Studios ("we"). We built it to collect nothing about the children who play it or the adults who install it.

Information we collect

None. The app does not collect, store on our servers, share or sell any personal or non-personal information. Specifically, the app has:

  • no accounts, sign-in or registration;
  • no advertising and no advertising identifiers;
  • no analytics, crash reporting or tracking tools;
  • no in-app purchases;
  • no chat, social features or links to other apps or websites;
  • no access to location, camera, microphone, contacts or photos.

The Android app does not request the Internet permission, so it cannot send information anywhere.

Information stored on the device

To remember progress, the app saves game data only on the device: the chosen hero, coins, items, math level, scores and the name of each save slot (which a child may type, for example a first name or nickname). This data never leaves the device. It is erased when a save slot is deleted in the game, when the app's storage is cleared, or when the app is uninstalled. Android's own device backup may include this data if the device owner has turned backup on; that backup is handled by Google under the device owner's account, not by us.

Children's privacy

The app is designed for children and complies with the U.S. Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA), the EU/UK GDPR and Google Play's Families Policy. Because we collect no personal information from anyone, there is no information to review, correct or delete on our side.

Third parties

The app contains no third-party SDKs that collect data. It is built with the open-source Capacitor framework, which runs the game locally on the device.

Changes to this policy

If we ever change what the app does with information, we will update this page and the date above before the change is released, and we will not begin collecting children's personal information without first meeting the legal requirements, including verifiable parental consent where required.
